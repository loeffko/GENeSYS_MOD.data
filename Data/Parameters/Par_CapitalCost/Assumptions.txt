NA renewables cost basis (decisions 2026-08-02, after NREL ATB 2024 / EIA
AEO2025 benchmarking):
	- Onshore wind: 2025 anchor kept from the FEL2026 LCOE model
	  (1563 EUR/kW); the DECLINE follows the NREL ATB 2024 learning curve
	  (index 1.000 / 0.897 / 0.804 at 2025 / 2030 / 2040) rather than the
	  LCOE file's flatter -9%. ATB's absolute levels (1426/1279/1146) were
	  not adopted: the ATB-vs-LCOE spread is modest for onshore and the
	  2025 anchor is considered sound. NOTE: ATB and EIA both put US
	  onshore fixed O&M near 30 EUR/kW/yr, ~40% above the value used here
	  (21.6) - open item.
	- Offshore wind: LCOE-model values kept (4571 / 4700 / 4040 EUR/kW at
	  2025 / 2030 / 2040), including the RISE from 2025 to 2030. This is
	  deliberate, not a data error: the US offshore pipeline is currently
	  stalled, so the learning-curve declines that ATB projects through
	  2030 have no basis in installed volume. Costs are held elevated while
	  the pipeline is frozen and decline afterwards as it restarts. ATB
	  Moderate (5105 / 3865 / 2871) and EIA (3412 for 2025) differ by ~50%
	  from each other, mainly over export-cable and interconnection scope,
	  so this parameter is genuinely uncertain; the values used sit between
	  the two sources in the start year. Fixed O&M kept at 132/123/105
	  (EIA ~142 supports this; ATB's ~80 looks low).
	- Utility PV: under review (the previously cited "EIA backup 680.6
	  EUR/kW" is NOT supported by the actual EIA source, which gives
	  ~1389 EUR/kW for 2025; ATB 2024 agrees at ~1387). Level to be
	  re-anchored on empirical US installed-price data; the concern with
	  ATB/EIA is that they embed module import tariffs whose persistence
	  to 2040 is a policy assumption rather than a measurement.

	- Utility PV RESOLVED 2026-08-02 (option B, faster premium erosion):
	  1245 / 980 / 600 EUR/kW-AC at 2025 / 2030 / 2040 (linear between),
	  fixed O&M 19 / 16 / 13 EUR/kW-AC/yr.
	  2025 anchor = LBNL Utility-Scale Solar 2025 ed., >250 MW-AC cohort
	  (1.38 USD/W-AC, 2024 COD) - the empirical price of the large projects a
	  capacity-expansion model actually builds, vs a 1.61 USD/W-AC fleet mean.
	  2040 = NREL ATB 2024 ADVANCED case, i.e. the global-module-spot-price
	  basis: assumes the US cost premium erodes essentially fully by 2040.
	  Cross-checks for 2025 (EUR2023/kW-AC): EIA/S&L AEO2025 1389, ATB 2024
	  Moderate 1381, NREL benchmark 2024 1370, IRENA US 1260, SEIA/WoodMac
	  Q4-2025 1564. Conversion: US GDP implicit deflator to 2023 + ECB 2023
	  average 1.0813 USD/EUR. Fixed O&M is ATB full scope (system + property
	  tax/insurance/admin), consistent with EIA/S&L 18.7; use 13/11/9 instead
	  if land lease and property tax are ever booked elsewhere.
	  WHY THE PREVIOUS VALUE WAS WRONG: the "EIA backup 680.6 EUR/kW" taken
	  from the LCOE file is IRENA's GLOBAL average on a DC basis
	  (~624 EUR2023/kW-DC). Using it for the US on an AC basis stacked three
	  errors (region: US is ~1.53x the global average per IRENA; denominator:
	  ILR 1.32-1.34; deflator). 650-700 EUR/kW-AC implies 546-588 USD/kW-DC,
	  below China and at India's level.
	  ON TARIFFS: the US premium is structural, not policy. Rebuilding NREL's
	  own PV cost model (PVSCM, Q1-2025) gives gross tariff content
	  6.2 ct/W-DC (5.6% of price) against a 45X pass-through of -7.5 ct/W-DC,
	  i.e. a NET policy effect near zero; ATB out-year projections are
	  tariff-free by construction. Fraunhofer ISE puts the genuine US
	  manufacturing-cost disadvantage at 12.8 EURct/Wp (labour 3-4x,
	  equipment capex +40-70%). Option B assumes this erodes fully by 2040 -
	  the optimistic end of the defensible range, and the reason the 2040
	  value sits below every Moderate-case projection.
	  RISK FLAGS: observed direction of travel is currently UP, not down
	  (LBNL real capex flat-to-rising 2022-24; SEIA/WoodMac +14% through 2025;
	  Lazard LCOE +18% v18->v19), and a Section 232 polysilicon proclamation
	  (signed 2026-08-06, effective 2026-12-04) sets a 0.38 USD/W-DC minimum
	  module import price, above current import prices. 45X also phases out
	  2030-2032, removing the credit that currently offsets tariffs.
	  KNOWN OMISSION: these are UNSUBSIDISED costs. Real US projects carry a
	  30-50% investment tax credit, and the investment model has no
	  investment-subsidy mechanism (the IRA bid adders act in dispatch only),
	  so PV build-out is biased low until an ITC representation exists.
